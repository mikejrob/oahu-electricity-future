# Does HSEO's study address the land its scenarios require?

Review prepared 2026-07-28 for the DRAFT_v7 pre-release check. Not committed.

## Document reviewed

- **HSEO, "Alternative Fuel, Repowering, and Energy Transition Study," revised May 2026, full report with appendices** (322-page PDF).
  URL: energy.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/HSEO-Alternative-Fuels-Study-Revised-May-2026-w-Appendices.pdf
  Landing page: energy.hawaii.gov/alternative-fuels-repowering-and-energy-transition-study/
- **LCCA workbook** `HSEO_AltFuels_Rev_LCCA_May 2026.xlsm` (from HSEO_AltFuels_Rev_LCCA_May-2026-1.zip on the same page).

## What HSEO says about land

1. **One acreage estimate, attached to Hawaiian Electric's plan, not to HSEO's own scenarios.** The executive summary notes Oʻahu's "limited land availability" (p. 6), then states that the IGP Preferred Base Scenario's 3,300 MW of utility-scale solar, at an assumed 0.15 MW/acre, "will require approximately 22,000 acres of land, occupying approximately 90% of the technically feasible land estimated to be available for utility-scale solar energy production," citing NREL's technical potential assessment (Grue, Waechter, Williams & Lockshin, updated July 2021) (pp. 6–7, footnotes 8–9).
2. **An illustrative map, with a disclaimer.** Figure 3 (p. 7) shades the 22,000 acres over the "Alt-1 Technical Feasibility Area" and states the figure "is for illustrative purposes only, technical potential does not indicate where solar will be sited." The study adds that developing this land "will take time, requires careful planning to address a broad range of land-use concerns" (pp. 6–7). The land-use concerns are not enumerated or analyzed anywhere in the study.
3. **An inherited screen in the scenario model.** The Engage capacity-expansion appendix caps solar and onshore wind at the technical potential in Hawaiian Electric's 2023 IGP Base workbooks, which "uses the Alt-1 land exclusions outlined in the 2021 update of the NREL technical potential report" (Engage appendix, PDF p. 211). The study does not state what the Alt-1 exclusions are (slope thresholds, ag classes, or other criteria), test them, or convert its own scenario solar builds to acres.
4. **Offshore wind by assumption.** Offshore wind enters the Engage runs capped at 400 MW, available from 2035, with 2023-ATB-derived PPA costs; "The model preferred offshore wind over other resources and imposed offshore wind constraints (400 MW or 0 MW) have a noticeable impact on results" (PDF pp. 211–212). There is no siting, cable, port, or permitting analysis for offshore wind in the study, and HSEO's own Ocean Energy Fact Sheet (Oct 2025) does not treat it as a near-term resource.
5. **Land arguments deployed against biofuels, not applied to solar siting.** The study quantifies land-use efficiency where it argues against local biodiesel: solar is "67 times more land-efficient" than common biodiesel feedstocks (p. 21, Figure 7); replacing 5% of statewide consumption with palm-oil biodiesel would need over 86,000 acres of new cropland (p. 46). Land also appears in a findings bullet, "Land availability and other factors indicate that local energy supply is insufficient to meet both current and forecasted demand" (p. 15), in plant-siting criteria that favor existing plant sites to minimize "land use impacts" and "community disruptions and the potential for land-use issues" (pp. 12, 29, Table 2), and in a generic list of delay factors including "land-use approvals" and "community opposition and concerns regarding land use, cultural impacts, and environmental preservation" (p. 20).

## What HSEO omits

- No acreage for its own scenarios. The 22,000-acre figure describes HECO's IGP buildout; HSEO's Engage scenario tables report GWh and MW with no land conversion.
- No independent land screen. The feasibility layer is inherited from NREL 2021 via HECO's workbooks; its criteria are neither stated nor examined. There is no slope analysis, no treatment of LSB agricultural classes for solar (an LCC reference appears only for energy crops, p. 41), no discussion of the Act 55 B/C caps or the special-use-permit pathway, and no parcel-level or transmission-proximity work.
- No follow-through on its own 90% statement. Taken at face value, the statement implies the IGP buildout nearly exhausts feasible solar land. The study does not ask whether the screen is conservative, what binds if it is not, or how its scenarios change if the ceiling is real. The number is stated once and not used.
- Community acceptance is invoked (pp. 12, 20) but never analyzed for solar siting.

## Fairness check on our current text, and corrections needed

1. **"PLEXOS" is the wrong label — correction required.** HSEO's scenario tool is Engage, an NREL-hosted model built on Calliope (Engage appendix, PDF p. 206, fn. 2). The LCCA workbook's own TOC says "Contains output from NREL ENGAGE model" and its Energy.RawData sheet is sourced "NREL ENGAGE Model output, interpolated by HDR." The string "PLEXOS" does not appear anywhere in the 322-page study or the LCCA workbook. Our report says "[HSEO PLEXOS workbooks]" (§2.6, line ~743) and "PLEXOS-priced full-scenario fuel gap" (Appendix A.6). Both should read Engage (or "HSEO's Engage scenario tables / LCCA workbook"). PLEXOS is a HECO IGP production-cost tool; attributing it to HSEO is the kind of error a state-agency reader would catch.
2. **"They also do not detail what land is and isn't available for solar or why" (§2.6, line ~734) is too strong as written.** HSEO does point to a specific feasibility layer (NREL Alt-1) and does attach an acreage figure to the IGP buildout. The defensible version: HSEO cites an inherited screen but does not state its criteria, examine it, attach acreage to its own scenarios, or analyze slopes, soil classes, or the legal pathways.
3. **"The solar pathway is not optimized; it is fixed, assumed pathway" (§2.6, lines ~734–736) needs qualification.** The Engage scenario analysis is a least-cost optimization subject to RPS constraints and the inherited technical-potential caps (PDF pp. 206, 212). What is fixed is the LCCA calculator's generation series, which is Engage output "interpolated by HDR" with engineering adjustments (LCCA Energy.RawData source note). Direction of effect: this correction weakens our "not optimized" framing somewhat. The substantive point survives in a different form: the solar quantity is bounded by an inherited land screen the study never examines, and the model's preference for offshore wind rests on assumed costs and an assumed 400 MW cap with no siting analysis.
4. **The 2050 generation numbers we quote (4,570 / 2,536 / 1,678 / 3,117 / 3,294 GWh) could not be re-verified in this pass.** The LCCA raw-data table ends at 2045 and covers the LNG pathway only; the Engage O'ahu results tables (PDF pp. 213–218) are images with no text layer. If these figures came from those tables or the Tableau dashboard, the A.6 citation should name that source explicitly. The author should confirm the source before release.
5. **HSEO's 90% statement cuts against our land-availability thesis and the report should engage it directly rather than leave it uncited.** At HSEO's density (0.15 MW/acre), 22,000 acres = 90% of feasible implies a feasibility layer of roughly 24,400 acres, about 3,700 MW. Our §2.5 screen finds 27,256 buildable acres for 5,451 MW at 5 acres/MW. The gap is a density assumption (6.67 vs 5 acres/MW) plus screen scope (our graduated slope bands to 30% and the B/C legal-pathway draw versus NREL's unexamined Alt-1 exclusions). Naming this disagreement, with both sources, is stronger than omitting the 90% figure and risking the appearance that we ignored it.

## Suggested text for the report (§2.6, replacing lines ~733–736)

> HSEO does assume much less grid-scale solar in total. Its study registers the land question without analyzing it: the executive summary estimates that the 3,300 MW of utility solar in Hawaiian Electric's Integrated Grid Plan would occupy about 22,000 acres, "approximately 90% of the technically feasible land" for utility solar on Oʻahu, citing NREL's 2021 technical-potential assessment (study pp. 6–7). That feasibility screen is inherited from the utility's planning workbooks (the NREL "Alt-1" land exclusions; Engage appendix), and the study does not state its criteria, test its sensitivity, or attach acreage to its own scenarios' solar builds. At HSEO's density assumption the screen implies roughly 3,700 MW of feasible utility solar; our parcel-level screen in Section 2.5, which grades slope to 30 percent and prices the statutory pathways for B/C cropland, finds 5,451 MW on 27,256 acres. The difference is a land-availability judgment, and ours is stated in checkable form: which parcels, at what slope, under which rule.

Companion fixes: change "[HSEO PLEXOS workbooks]" to "[HSEO Engage scenario tables]" (§2.6) and "PLEXOS-priced" to "Engage-priced" or "scenario-priced" (A.6), and soften "it is fixed, assumed pathway" to reflect that the Engage runs optimize subject to the inherited caps while the LCCA series is fixed.

## Bottom line

HSEO's study does not adequately address the land its own scenarios raise. It states the sharpest land fact in the official record (90% of technically feasible land consumed by the IGP buildout) and then neither defends the screen behind it nor applies it to its own scenarios; its land quantification is reserved for the case against biodiesel crops. Our characterization is directionally fair but needs three fixes before release: the PLEXOS-to-Engage relabel, the qualification of "do not detail what land is available" and "not optimized," and a citation check on the 2050 generation figures.
