# Hawaiian Electric Adequacy of Supply filings

Annual filings to the Hawaiʻi Public Utilities Commission. These are the
primary source for the forward-looking EFORd rates and four-year actuals in
report Table 6.1 and the Section 6.3 discussion.

Where the report's numbers sit in these documents:

- **Forward EFORd by unit (Table 3)**: 2021 filing p. 10; 2022 filing p. 9.
  Kahe 1–4 and Waiau 7–8 at 10%, Kahe 5–6 at 7%, Waiau 9–10 at 8.2%,
  CIP CT-1 at 4.2%, Schofield (SGS) 1–6 at 2%. Waiau 3 at 25% both years;
  Waiau 4 at 18% (2021) and 25% (2022). Only utility-owned units appear;
  independent producers are absent from both tables.
- **Four-year actuals and adjustments**: 2021 filing Appendix 4
  (pp. 23–24); 2022 filing Appendix 2 (pp. 16–17). Kahe 1–4 + Waiau 7–8
  reported as one group (14.4% in 2021, 14.2% in 2022, adjusted to 10% for
  one-time events and pandemic-disrupted maintenance); Kahe 5–6 12.3% /
  13%; Waiau 9–10 10% / 11.3%; CIP CT-1 4.2% (the four-year average
  itself). Waiau 3's 39% four-year actual is in the 2021 filing, p. 23 —
  a 74-year-old "limited use" unit, not averaged with any other, retired
  in 2024 and not in the model.
- **The cycling sentence**: "the generating units are being operated in
  ways for which they were not designed" — 2021 filing p. 9; repeated
  verbatim in the 2022 filing p. 8.

The filings' horizons are five years (2021–2025, 2022–2026) with rates
held flat. The post-2027 escalation used in HSEO's PLEXOS workbook
(`sources/plan_mix/PLEXOS_24-10-29_cost_calculations_MF/ForcedOutageRate-Table 1.csv`,
e.g. Kahe 1–4 to 13%) is HSEO's modeling assumption, not part of these
filings.
